TETRA Digital Clusters: Why 800MHz Still Matters

August 2026 · Radio systems

Notes from maintaining TETRA systems at several Chinese ports — Yingkou, Yantai, Lianyungang, Xianrendao — and why 800MHz digital trunking still holds its place there.

Why 800 MHz wins at a port

Penetration matters. A port is one of the densest metal environments around — stacked containers, cranes, sheds. A lower band keeps the link alive where higher frequencies start bouncing.

Reach matters too. A single site can cover a radius of 10 to 30 km, which cuts the number of base stations needed, and with it the cost of building and maintaining the network.

Why the industry still needs it

Port dispatch asks a lot of voice communication. Group call and priority dispatch map directly onto critical operations: the question is not whether a call can be placed, but whether an instruction reaches everyone at once.

PDT is already widely deployed across domestic public security systems, so experience built on the TETRA standard transfers, which lowers the marginal cost of staying with it.

Keeping it alive

VSWR monitoring is the backbone of preventive maintenance. It surfaces the parts that age first — antennas, feeders — before the link actually fails, and the VT100 logs are where that measurement lives.

Automating the inspection pipeline compresses hours of manual log parsing into minutes. What that buys is not typing time: it makes a full check on every site, every round, actually feasible.

Not yet verified

The exact number of sites and the covered area need to be checked against the latest maintenance records before they are written down. The method and the judgement are what this piece is about.
