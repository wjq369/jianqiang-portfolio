The Organon and Modern Logic

September 2026 · Reading notes

How Aristotle's six logical works fit together, what each one says it is doing, and where we still meet them more than two thousand years later.

What is in the Organon

The Organon collects six works on logic: Categories, On Interpretation, Prior Analytics, Posterior Analytics, Topics and Sophistical Refutations, together with a translator's afterword and notes. The line runs from words and categories through propositions, syllogism and demonstration to dialectical reasoning and the diagnosis of fallacies.

What each work says it is for

  • On Interpretation: first to settle nouns, verbs, negation, affirmation, statement and sentence.
  • Prior Analytics: the subject is demonstration, and the content is coming to understand it.
  • Posterior Analytics: it opens from the claim that all instruction and all intellectual learning comes from pre-existing knowledge.
  • Topics: to find a method of inquiry that reasons from reputable opinions.
  • Sophistical Refutations: arguments that look like refutations but are actually fallacies.
  • Categories has no such opening statement; it starts straight in with homonyms, synonyms and derivatives.

Why syllogism comes before proof

The text gives its own reason for the ordering: syllogism is discussed before demonstration because it is the more general thing. A demonstration is a kind of syllogism, but not every syllogism is a demonstration.

A framework sometimes called big logic

The translator's own assessment — second-hand reading, not Aristotle's text — is that the Organon sets up a framework running from concept to judgement to inference to argument to the basic laws of thought, and that its main logical achievement is a term logic built around categorical propositions and the theory of the syllogism.

Notes on this edition

The edition read here is the Chinese translation published by Shanghai People's Publishing House in August 2018, made from the revised Oxford translation of Jonathan Barnes's Complete Works of Aristotle — not directly from the Greek. The division of labour among translators, the final editing, and the history of the project are all set out in the book.

Open questions

Aristotle's dates, birthplace and teachers cannot be found in the text of this translation, and I have not filled them in from memory. Nor does it explain where the collective name Organon comes from, or who edited the six works into the set and when.
